# Render work allowance: calibration (task 13.4a)

Read-only spike for row 13.4: what the renderer's counted work is over the corpus, in the two
candidate units, and what that says about the unit, the default and the caller's view of a
stopped page. Nothing under `SafePdf/` changed. No time is measured: counted work is
deterministic (two full runs, with and without `--check`, printed identical numbers).

## What is measured

* **steps**: `RState.steps` of the final state of `Glyph.renderItems`, i.e. the quantity that
  `renderItemsCounted_steps_le` (`Core/Raster/Clip.lean`: at most the sum of `itemCost`),
  `fillEdgesCounted_steps_le` (`Core/Raster/Fill.lean`: at most `h · (S·E + w + 1)`) and the
  per-kind lemmas (`renderImage_steps_le`, ...) bound. It counts clip-mask levels (rasterizer
  rows plus mask area), fills, glyph masks, compositing (`r.width · r.height` per paint),
  images, shadings, tiling cells and Type 3 glyphs. The page's front half (`renderDocPage`
  up to `renderPage`) is repeated in the tool because `renderDocPage` returns only the image;
  `--check` verified the repeated pipeline paints the same image: **equal on 2,450 of 2,450
  pages**.
* **pixels**: pixels written: the area of every coverage mask built (each clip level's fill
  mask and its intersection, each fill, glyph and stroke mask) plus the area composited. It is
  `steps` without the rasterizer's edge-walking term `S · E` per row. No counter for it exists
  in the library; it comes from a replay of the top-level display items with the same
  functions (`clipMaskC`'s recursion on the real `ClipCache`, `fillCounted`, `runMasks`, the
  page stroker). Items that are not fills, strokes or glyph runs (images, shadings) and the
  Type 3 or pattern-coloured items are charged their real `steps` delta in both units.
  The replay's step total equals the real `steps` on **2,397 of 2,450 pages**; the other 53
  all have transparency groups (replay lower, by up to 45%; cause not investigated), so
  `pixels` is a lower bound there and `steps` is the authoritative number.
* Corpus: `/home/agent/workspace/safe-pdf/corpus/files`, 81 files (74 arXiv, 6 NTSB, 1 USCIS),
  **2,450 pages, 0 render errors**, page 1 to last, default `Limits`, no user rotation, no
  optional-content state, substitute fonts as the inspector loads them. 155 pages contain Type
  3 or pattern-coloured items, 150 pages have transparency groups (3 with soft masks).
* Not in the counter in either unit: the stroker's own work (a stroke is charged only its
  composite rectangle) and the transparency driver's layer buffers (the file counts no steps).
  `page-long-stroke` and `stroke-zigzag-10000` below show the first: 10,000 segments cost the
  same counted work as one line.

## Proposal

**(1) Unit: steps.** Reasons: it is what `renderItemsCounted_steps_le` and the per-kind lemmas
already bound, with no new counter and no new proof obligation on the definition; it contains
the edge-walking term, which the sliver fill shows is real work that pixels cannot see (and
`fillEdgesCounted_steps_le` is `h · (S·E + w + 1)`, an a-priori bound known from the edge
count and the mask rectangle before any row is walked); it already charges images, shadings,
tiling and Type 3 work in the same currency. Pixels separate the corpus from the attacks no
better (1.01x apart on them) and would need a counter built into every painter. Caveat that
holds for both units and is for 13.4b to settle: strokes are charged only their composite
(add the stroker's own work, or document that it is bounded separately by 7.2b's bound and
`maxDashSteps`).

**(2) Default allowance: `renderStepFactor = 128` steps per page pixel** (a field of `Limits`),
allowance `max 2^20 (128 · W · H)` for a page of `W x H` pixels (the floor, one Letter
full-page fill, keeps tiny pages workable). Numbers: Letter at 72 dpi gets 62,042,112
(4.06x the largest corpus page, 15,288,090), A4 at 72 dpi 64,234,496 (`128 · 501,832`),
Letter at 144 dpi 248,168,448 (6.91x the 144 dpi maximum, 35,904,099). **0 of
2,450 corpus pages hit it at 72 dpi and 0 at 144 dpi**; the tightest margin is the page with
31.54 steps per pixel at 72 dpi (4.06x). Why per pixel: the work of a corpus page grows with
the area (1.03x to 3.98x per 4x area), so a constant tuned at 72 dpi (the nearest power of
two above 4x the maximum is 2^26 = 67,108,864, 4.39x) leaves only 1.87x at 144 dpi and less
beyond; a constant tuned at the largest zoom lets a Letter page at 72 dpi spend many times
the corpus maximum. Against the inputs (Letter, 72 dpi, allowance 62.0M): a full-page-fills
file stops after about 63 fills (of 1,000: 15.7x over), clip-stack-2000-fills after about 43
levels with their fills (46.7x over), sliver-fill-5000 (32.6M) is under it. Margins and the corpus: the corpus
is 74 arXiv papers, 6 NTSB reports and 1 USCIS form (text, Type 3 bitmap fonts, vector
figures, few images); a map, CAD or scanned-image corpus would have heavier pages, so 128 is
a first value for papers and reports, not a measured bound for all documents, and `Limits`
lets an embedder raise it.

Two findings the allowance's design must respect (13.4b):

* **The check must sit inside an item's clip mask and fill, not only between items.**
  `clip-stack-2000.pdf` (the 2,000 clips and one fill at the end) is a single item whose clip
  list costs 1,448,339,178 steps (`clipCost`): a check between items would still let it run to
  the end. Charge per clip level (the recursion of `clipMaskC` stops when the allowance is
  spent) and per fill, so the render's work is at most the allowance plus one level or one fill
  (the second is up to `h · (S · E + w + 1)`: 32.6M for the sliver fill, about 6.5G for the
  same shape with a million slivers: this is arithmetic from the bound, not run; the a-priori
  bound is known after flattening, so the fill can be refused before its rows are walked).
  The theorem "work is at most the allowance plus one item's" of the row is too weak for the
  clip stack and should be stated per operation.
* **A hit must not depend on the cache.** `clipMaskC` charges 0 on a cache hit; the counter is
  what the renderer already charges, so a hit costs nothing and the bound stays a bound on
  work done.

**(3) What the caller sees when the allowance is hit.** Recommended: `limitExceeded` **and**
the pixels so far, marked as incomplete, never silent (the precedent is the op and form
limits, where the page "still renders what it has"). The core returns the image and the stop
reason together; each layer then decides:

* `Api.renderPage` keeps its signature `Except Error Image` and returns
  `.error (.pdf (.limitExceeded .renderWork))` (a new `LimitKind`, with its string in
  `Shell/Views/Common.renderLimitKind`), so `renderPage_size` still reads "ok means a complete
  page"; a new `Api.renderPagePartial` returns the image and whether it is complete, for
  clients that want to show something. The existing library callers see an error.
* Worker protocol: today `pageError` turns an error into `Response.error .pageFailed message`,
  which already carries a limit error with no protocol change (the error-only variant costs
  nothing there). For partial images add `Response.image` a completeness flag or a new variant
  (`partialImage`, new codec tag, round-trip test in `Protocol`); the response cap and
  `renderPage_size` apply unchanged (`4 · w · h` bytes).
* Viewers: the Tk viewer caches `Except Error Image` per page and shows an error text in place
  of the page; with a partial image it shows the raster and a status-line or banner message
  ("page too complex, drawn partly") and does not retry it (a retry is deterministic); the
  macOS/FFI shell and the inspector (`--view raster` JSON: `status` gets a third value) need
  the same flag. Error-only is the cheaper first step (no protocol change, one `LimitKind`,
  one string, the viewers' existing error page); the flag is the recommended end state.

Fallback if partial images are not wanted: error only, `limitExceeded .renderWork`; no
protocol, API or viewer-type change; the consequence is that a page with a legitimately heavy
section shows no page at all instead of most of it.
